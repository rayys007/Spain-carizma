Why Choose Spain Carizma

Most guidance on Spanish visas is written for people already in Europe. Our service is built for applicants abroad: your country's documents, its legalisation rules and the Spanish consulate that covers it.

Correct Document Legalisation

Hague Convention countries use an apostille; others go through foreign-ministry attestation and the Spanish consulate. We map every document to the right chain and sworn translation.

Filed at the Right Office

Your application goes to the Spanish consulate for your country of residence, or directly to Spain's fast-track unit for nomads, professionals and transfers, whichever is quicker for your case.

Visa and Business Together

Company formation, opening licences and trademark registration run in parallel with your permit, so your business is ready when you arrive.

Families Filed Together

Spouse, children and dependent parents included in the same submission wherever the route allows, with the income and housing evidence sized for the whole household.

Fixed Fees in Writing

Our fee and all official charges on one page before we begin. Government fees, translations and legalisation are passed through at cost.

Renewals Tracked

Your expiry dates are in our diary. We contact you before each renewal window opens so nothing lapses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to be in Spain to apply?

Usually not. Most routes are filed through the Spanish consulate for the country where you legally reside, and the nomad, highly qualified and intra-company routes can be filed directly in Spain by a representative. Your first trip can be the one where you move.

What is the difference between an apostille and legalisation?

If your document was issued in a country that belongs to the Hague Apostille Convention, a single apostille makes it valid in Spain. If not, it is attested by that country's foreign ministry and then legalised at the Spanish consulate. In either case it then requires a sworn translation into Spanish.

How long does the process take?

It depends on the route and on consulate appointment availability, which is often the longest part. Fast-track routes filed in Spain are usually quicker. We give you a realistic range for your own case at the first consultation.

Can my family come with me?

On most routes, yes, and on several the family applications are submitted at the same time as yours. Where a separate reunification file is required, we set out in advance the income and housing evidence it will need.

Can I keep my current job or company?

Often, yes. The Digital Nomad Visa is designed for exactly that, within a limit on Spanish-source income. Other routes have their own rules on work, and some, such as the non-lucrative visa, do not permit working in Spain. We confirm this before you choose a route.

How are fees structured?

Our fee depends on the route and the number of applicants on the file. It is fixed and confirmed in writing before we begin. Government fees, legalisation, translations, notary and registry charges are passed through at cost and itemised separately.

Is this legal advice?

We manage procedures and filings. Where a case requires a lawyer, a tax adviser or a technical architect, we say so and work alongside them.
